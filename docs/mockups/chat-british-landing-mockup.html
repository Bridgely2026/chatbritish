You ask your manager about a promotion. She says, “It's definitely something we can look at.” No date is mentioned.
What do you do?
1Take it as a near-term commitment and wait.
2Ask to set a date to revisit it, and what she'd want to see from you before then.
3Drop the subject so you don't seem pushy.
Not quite

It's a deferral, not a promise. With no date attached, nothing is actually in motion.

Money and transactions

Splitting the bill evenly

What was said
“Shall we just split it evenly?”
What it means
A casual default, not a rule. It's normal to say lightly that you had less.
What you could say
“I only had a starter. Would you mind if I paid a bit less?”

What they sayWhat it means
“Let's take that offline”This isn't necessarily dismissive. It can protect a sensitive point from being aired publicly, but it does require the person to actively follow up.
“Try the pharmacy first”This is a standard, expected referral for minor issues, not the surgery deflecting or downgrading the concern.
“I'll see what I can do”Often means low priority unless followed up. Not necessarily a refusal, but not a commitment either.
